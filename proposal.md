Brody Kretz

## 1. Research question

Across Department of Defense contract awards from 2020 through 2026, how do the biggest defense contractors differ in what they get paid for, and how dependent is each one on a single category of work or a single branch of the military? The answer is a profile for each company rather than a yes or no. For each one I want the share of its DoD dollars by product and service category, by branch, and by whether the work was competed or sole source. A strength is a category where a company holds a big share, and a weakness is one where it barely shows up. The companies are the five biggest primes, Lockheed Martin, RTX, Boeing, General Dynamics, and Northrop Grumman, plus the newer defense tech companies Palantir, Anduril, Shield AI, and Scale AI.

Defense tech startups would care because it shows where the primes are thin and worth going after. Analysts and investors would care because depending on one branch or one category is a risk they have to price. Reporters at outlets like Breaking Defense would care because where DoD money goes is their beat. And people like me deciding where to apply would care, since I work on AI agent systems and care about the software side. I expect the primes to be concentrated in hardware like aircraft, ships, and missiles, and the newer companies in software and services on fixed price deals. It would surprise me if the primes hold more of the software dollars than the new companies, or if Anduril is just as dependent on one branch as a prime.

## 2. Data sources and method

My main source is the USAspending API at https://api.usaspending.gov with docs at https://api.usaspending.gov/docs/endpoints. It is the official federal spending record under the DATA Act and needs no key. I will use the spending_by_award endpoint at https://api.usaspending.gov/api/v2/search/spending_by_award/ for individual contract records and the spending_by_category endpoints for psc, naics, awarding_subagency, and recipient for totals grouped by category. The filters I need are awarding agency set to Department of Defense, a 2020 through 2026 time period, award type codes A through D for contracts, the recipient search text or id for the company, and the extent competed and contract pricing codes. My second source is the daily contract announcements at https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/ where DoD posts a page every weekday listing each contract over about 7.5 million dollars as a paragraph of text. I will scrape a sample as a check on the API numbers and as a fallback if the keyword searches get noisy.

The API side is Chapter 11, Government Data APIs, meaning requests with a User-Agent header that names me and JSON into pandas. The announcements are Chapter 6, Parsing Static Web Pages, with BeautifulSoup and regex pulling the company and amount out of each paragraph. Chapter 2 covers the robots.txt check and the pause between requests. The scope is DoD prime contract awards from January 1, 2020 through September 30, 2026, which means category totals per fiscal year for each company, nine companies times seven years times four groupings, plus every individual award to those companies, likely tens of thousands of rows, plus one year of announcement pages, about 250. For the analysis I will make a stacked bar per company of its dollars by service category, a grid of companies by awarding branch, a concentration score per company, meaning the share of its dollars in its biggest category, a line of each company's DoD dollars by year, and a competed versus sole source share per company.

## 3. Ethics and feasibility

USAspending data is public by law under the DATA Act and the API's code is CC0, so collecting it is allowed outright. The API needs no key and its docs set no rate limit. Its robots.txt at https://www.usaspending.gov/robots.txt only disallows .php pages and query string URLs on the website, not the API. I will still send a User-Agent with my name and email, pause one second between requests, and cache every response. Defense.gov is public with no login, but I have not confirmed it accepts scripted requests yet, so I will read its robots.txt, keep to one request every two seconds, and drop the scrape if it refuses me. On personal information, the data is about companies and agencies, not people. USAspending lists some individuals like sole proprietors on small awards and I will not touch those. Every pull is limited to a fixed list of large companies, so no person's name lands in my dataset, and the announcement pages only name companies and cities.

For the one record, I sent a POST to https://api.usaspending.gov/api/v2/search/spending_by_award/ from a Jupyter notebook asking for the largest DoD award to Anduril since 2020, with Department of Defense as the awarding agency, 2020 through 2026 as the window, award types A through D, and Anduril as the recipient search text. The full cell and output are in the screenshot. It came back with status 200 and one record, award H9240224F0057, a 249,978,466 dollar delivery order from U.S. Special Operations Command to Anduril Industries for Roadrunner, Anvil and Quasar packages, NAICS 334511 and PSC 5340 Hardware, Commercial.

The fields I will use are Award ID, Recipient Name, Award Amount, Start Date, Awarding Sub Agency, Description, NAICS, PSC, Contract Award Type, and recipient_id, which is the key for the category totals. The recipient_id here ends in C for a child entity, and the matching parent id ending in P is what I will use to roll subsidiaries up. That is also the biggest risk, company names. Lockheed Martin shows up under many legal entities and the recipient search matches loosely, so a sloppy search splits a company's dollars or pulls in the wrong ones. My fallback is that parent id, and if that still looks wrong I cut the list to the six cleanest names. The second risk is that the service codes are coarse and spread software work across several codes. My fallback there is to group the codes into five or six buckets by hand, and if time allows classify the descriptions with an LLM and check a random sample against my own coding, as Chapter 13 and Chapter 15 say to.
